Research

Thermoelectrics

Heat-to-electricity conversion

Research focus

Designing semiconductors that convert waste heat into useful electrical energy.

Synthesis, crystal growth and transport measurements connect composition and structure to thermoelectric performance. We combine electronic-structure design, phonon scattering and intrinsically low thermal conductivity across PbTe, SnSe and related chalcogenides.

  • PbTe
  • SnSe
  • PbSe & PbS
  • Bi₂Te₃
Featured: Representative thermoelectric studies ↓Read the original research description ↗

Materials design

Move charge efficiently. Slow the flow of heat.

A useful thermoelectric combines a large Seebeck coefficient, strong electrical conductivity and low thermal conductivity. Our research uses chemistry to balance these coupled properties through composition, bonding and structure.

zT = S2σT / κ

S is the Seebeck coefficient, σ the electrical conductivity, T the absolute temperature and κ the total thermal conductivity.

01 / Design strategy

Engineer electronic structure

Tune carrier concentration and band structure to strengthen the power factor without sacrificing electrical transport.

02 / Design strategy

Scatter heat-carrying phonons

Build atomic-, nano-, and mesoscale features that impede lattice vibrations across a broad range of length scales.

03 / Design strategy

Discover low-κ crystals

Search for crystal structures whose bonding and anharmonicity naturally suppress lattice thermal conductivity.

2011 Research highlight

Combining electronic and nanoscale design

High Performance Na-doped PbTe–PbS Thermoelectric Materials: Electronic Density of States Modification and Shape-Controlled Nanostructures ↗

Journal of the American Chemical Society 133(41), 16588–16597 (2011)

Reported resultzT 1.8 at 800 K

In PbTe containing 12% PbS and 2% Na doping, controlled PbS precipitates suppress lattice heat transport, while changes in the electronic density of states improve the power factor.

Why it matters Shows how one chemical system can improve electrical transport and reduce heat conduction together.

2012 Research highlight

Scattering phonons at every scale

High-performance bulk thermoelectrics with all-scale hierarchical architectures ↗

Nature 489, 414–418 (2012)

Reported resultzT ≈ 2.2 at 915 K

PbTe–SrTe combines atomic disorder, nanoscale precipitates, and mesoscale grain boundaries. These features scatter heat-carrying phonons over different length scales while retaining useful electrical transport.

Why it matters Established a multiscale design strategy that connects chemistry, microstructure, and bulk thermoelectric performance.

Read paper ↗

2014 Research highlight

Letting the crystal itself block heat

Ultralow thermal conductivity and high thermoelectric figure of merit in SnSe crystals ↗

Nature 508, 373–377 (2014)

Reported resultzT 2.6 ± 0.3 at 923 K · b axis

SnSe single crystals exhibit strongly anharmonic, direction-dependent bonding and exceptionally weak lattice heat conduction. The reported performance along the b axis highlights the importance of crystallographic direction.

Why it matters Opened a route to high performance through intrinsic bonding and crystal structure, expanding the search for lead- and tellurium-free materials.

Read paper ↗

2021 Research highlight

Unlocking polycrystalline SnSe

Polycrystalline SnSe with a thermoelectric figure of merit greater than the single crystal ↗

Nature Materials 20(10), 1378–1384 (2021)

Reported resultzT ≈ 3.1 at 783 K

Trace tin oxides on SnSe powders were identified as a major obstacle to performance. Purifying the reagents and removing these oxides allowed hole-doped polycrystalline samples to combine better electrical transport with very low lattice thermal conductivity.

Why it matters Makes chemical purity and surface control central to developing high-performance polycrystalline SnSe.

Read paper ↗

2025 Research highlight

Local gaps in a cubic semiconductor

Stabilization of high-performance rock-salt LiMnSbTe3 thermoelectrics with embedded van der Waals-like gaps ↗

Nature Communications 16, Article 11501 (2025)

Reported resultzT 1.5 at 873 K · Mn-deficient composition

Combining MnTe and LiSbTe₂ produces a cubic semiconductor containing locally ordered, van der Waals-like gaps. These internal features scatter phonons while supporting carrier transport; adjusting the Mn content further improves performance.

Why it matters Extends selective phonon scattering to a new compound by combining a high-symmetry host with distinctive local structure.

Read paper ↗

2026 Research highlight

Structural complexity for glasslike heat transport

Structural Heterogeneity in Medium-Entropy AgMnSbPbTe4 for Glassy Thermal Transport and High Thermoelectric Performance ↗

Journal of the American Chemical Society 148(2), 2456–2470 (2026)

Reported resultPeak zT 1.72 at 800 K

AgMnSbPbTe₄ contains nanoscale precipitates and local strain within a compositionally uniform bulk material. This structural complexity strongly limits lattice heat transport, while band convergence and preserved carrier mobility support electrical performance.

Why it matters Connects medium-entropy chemistry with local structural control as a route to balancing electron and phonon transport.

Read paper ↗
